# Welcome to the third installment of the “AI Plumbers” DevRoom!

The AI Plumbers DevRoom started life in 2025 as the “Low-level AI Engineering
and Hacking” DevRoom, and took its current name in 2026. We aspire for this
DevRoom to become as important to the AI community as the Linux Plumbers
Conference is to the Linux community. We are bringing together the developers
working on the essential “plumbing” of the AI industry: hardware accelerators,
compilers and math kernel libraries, model quantization techniques, inference
runtimes and servers, fine-tuning and training engines, edge and physical AI,
distributed and rack-scale computing, and more. Together, we will spend the day
discussing core designs and collaborating to solve governance problems.

This year we are delighted to welcome new co-organizers from Hugging Face, Red
Hat and Roofline AI, alongside the organizers from previous years.

What is FOSDEM?
===============

FOSDEM is one of the world’s largest gatherings of Free Software contributors
and happens each year in Brussels (Belgium) at the ULB Campus Solbosch. In 2027,

What is the AI Plumbers DevRoom?
================================
This DevRoom is a celebration of Open Source low-level AI projects of all types
and sizes – the “plumbing” of the AI industry. It is a place for downstream open
source builders to meet the unsung heroes who thanklessly maintain the
“load-bearing” technologies powering much of the AI ecosystem. We invite project
contributors to present their features, architecture, design, real-world use
cases, and integrations. We encourage end users to share how open source
low-level AI projects are helping them solve their everyday challenges.

Ultimately, we hope that this DevRoom will be a chance to identify collaboration
opportunities between projects, and to discuss building opinionated AI stacks.

We believe in the individual open source programmer. You don't need a
megacorporation's budget to make significant breakthroughs in how we put open
and weight-available AI models to good use. With judicious use of AI-assisted
coding, a single open source hacker can make a real difference to the state of
the art.

We are looking for maintainers and committers of core low-level AI projects,
downstream projects building on top of them, and end users of AI stacks. If you
are wondering what type of projects are of interest, here are some examples we’d
love to hear about:
* Bare metal and edge AI: ExecuTorch, LiteRT, IREE
* Physical AI: LeRobot/Pollen Robotics, Dronecode, openpi
* AI compilers and kernel frameworks: PyTorch, MLIR/IREE, Mojo, Triton,
  BLIS/BLAS, Kompute/Vulkan
* Inference frameworks: ggml/llama.cpp, tinygrad, zml
* Inference servers: Triton/Dynamo, Docker Model Runner, Paddler, ramalama
* Open source agentic coding harnesses and local coding agents: pi, Shelley,
  Cyclo
* Open weights model training and fine-tuning: Bielik, Allen Institute (OLMo),
  Hugging Face, Unsloth
* RISC-V based open source AI accelerator hardware: AIFoundry, Tenstorrent,
  MosaicSoC

...and many, many more.

In previous years we had a bias towards inference, fine-tuning and quantization
over training. This year we are widening our scope: open weights training is
very welcome, as long as the focus is on the open source tooling that makes it
possible outside of the largest labs. We still love Edge AI.

This Devroom is a safe space for everyone, so we kindly ask all participants to
follow the FOSDEM code of conduct:
* https://fosdem.org/2027/practical/conduct/

We strongly encourage submissions from individuals of all genders, ethnicities,
abilities, and backgrounds to foster a diverse and inclusive environment. We
also welcome first-time speakers and, if needed, we will be happy to help you
rehearse your talk before the event.

Desirable topics
================
We would like to invite all FOSDEM participants to share the joys of hands-on,
low-level AI hacking (as opposed to simply consuming AI technology). Topics we
have in mind include:

**Edge, bare metal and physical AI**
* AI on minimal resource targets (bare metal/RTOS on microcontroller and DSP
  class processors)
* AI for image, video, audio and sensor data on small devices (MobileNet, YOLO,
  ...)
* Physical AI: drones, robotics and other AI beyond LLMs

**Hardware**
* Open source AI acceleration hardware (e.g. PULP Neureka:
  https://github.com/pulp-platform/neureka)
* Advances in specialized hardware acceleration (ASICs, RISC-V based
  accelerators, etc.)
* GPGPU computing and accelerators (Vulkan, Kompute, ...)

**Compilers, kernels and runtimes**
* IREE and other MLIR based AI compilers
* Matmul kernels in various libraries (BLAS, cuBLAS, Auto-TVM)
* Open source AI inference engines and servers
* Efficiency of AI runtimes: speed, code size and energy usage
* Advanced topics in model quantization

**Training, fine-tuning and scale**
* Open weights model training and fine-tuning on modest hardware
* Distributed computing for AI
* Data preparation frameworks and how they map to data engineering practices
* Large-scale performance analysis and debugging

**Beyond neural networks**
* Non-neural network AI frameworks (Bayesian inference, genetic programming,
  etc.)

**Tools and community**
* Open source agentic coding harnesses and local coding agents, and how they are
  changing low-level AI development
* The “documentation gap”: how do we make open source AI platforms accessible to
  new users?
* Governance of upstream-downstream relationships (e.g. llama.cpp and its
  downstream consumers, balkanization of the llama.cpp/ggml landscape, ...)
* The use of free and open source software in the low-level AI engineering
  community

We'd like to make the devroom as diverse as possible, so we are looking to offer
a mixture of presentations, short tutorials, demos, and discussions.
Presentations must be related to open source software, but we do encourage
submissions that push boundaries and experiment with the unexpected. We’d like
to hear about new developments in:
* optimizing AI workflows for low cost and small hardware
* optimizing AI pipelines to reduce energy consumption
* tooling and methods that make individuals hacking on AI more efficient, so
  that they can compete with the “throw your money at all the hardware you can
  get” approach
* making the most of existing SDKs, hardware and tooling to focus on doing AI
  without the overhead of setting things up

As well as more off-the-beaten-track topics. Here are a few ideas:
* Why does being able to “hack” AI matter?
* Are we at the mercy of big corporations with a lot of money when it comes to
  AI?
* Progress and possible upcoming contenders in the “hardware wars” for AI

Or, of course, a superposition of the above! Proposed talks should focus on
substance over AI hype. Please make your talk accessible to people who are not
experts in your particular domain: the focus should be on the software and
hardware, not on the underlying science.

If you have a wild idea that doesn’t fit into these categories, perfect—we love
surprises! If it’s weird, bold, or thought-provoking, we want to hear about it.

Gory details of talk submission
===============================

All submissions must be made via the FOSDEM Pretalx event planning site:
* https://pretalx.fosdem.org/fosdem-2027/cfp <!-- TODO: not up yet -->

If you already have a Pretalx account from a previous FOSDEM, please don't
create a new one. To reset your password, go to the Pretalx front page and
request a password reset.

During submission please make sure to select “AI Plumbers” from the Track list.
Please provide a meaningful abstract and description of your proposed session.

We expect more proposals than we can possibly accept, so it is vitally important
that you submit your proposal on or before the deadline. Please note that the
submission deadline is managed by the FOSDEM team and not by the devroom
managers, which means that after that deadline nothing will be accepted. This is
non-negotiable.

The communication language of the devroom is English. All content must relate to
free and open source software. All presentations will be recorded and made
available under Creative Commons licenses. In the Submission notes field, please
indicate that you agree that your presentation will be licensed under the
CC-By-SA-4.0 or CC-By-4.0 license and that you agree to have your presentation
recorded. For example:

`"If my presentation is accepted for FOSDEM, I hereby agree to license
all recordings, slides, and other associated materials under the
Creative Commons Attribution Share-Alike 4.0 International License.
Sincerely, <NAME>."`

Once you’re on the FOSDEM 2027 Pretalx website:
* Make sure the title and subtitle of your talk are descriptive, as titles will
  be listed alongside ~500 from other projects
* Select "AI Plumbers" as the track.
* Select "Talk" event type
* Provide a short abstract of one paragraph
* Provide a longer description if you wish to do so
* Make sure there is a short bio and contact information
* Add links to related websites / blogs etc.
* Make sure to indicate your desired talk duration. This year we welcome talks
  that are either
  * 10 min (short introduction of project/topic)
  * 20 min (normal slot)
  * 40 min (extraordinary slot for a topic that needs a deeper explanation)
  <!-- TODO: agree slot lengths with co-organizers. 2026 used 10/25/50; the 2027
       proposal states a strong preference for ~20 min talks. -->

Note that we may negotiate your talk duration down with you, since we are almost
guaranteed to receive more proposals than the day allows.
* If you plan to register your proposal in several tracks to increase your
  chances, DON’T! Register your talk once, in the most accurate track. If our
  devroom is not your first choice, just let us know, and give us a chance to
  save you a slot in case of rejection by the primary devroom. We coordinate
  AI-related submissions with the RISC-V and HPC devrooms, so a good talk
  submitted to the "wrong" one of the three will still find a home.
* For accepted talks:
  * You will receive an email to tell you that we accept your proposal
  * Expect additional emails with more instructions

Volunteering & Sponsorships

FOSDEM needs you! Every year, the FOSDEM team is assisted by an enthusiastic
team of volunteers to help out with various tasks and make the event a fun and
safe place for all our visitors.

There are some benefits to being a volunteer, but most importantly you help make

Whether or not you submit a talk, please consider volunteering if you plan on
